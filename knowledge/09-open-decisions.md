# Open decisions

Things the team still has to decide. Silence on any of these is worse than either answer.

## 1. Which artifact are we actually building next?
Roughly eleven days to a six-slide PDF; three months to a 36-hour build. The project plan
currently plans only the second. These reward almost opposite things.

## 2. Team size
The plan's split allocates six to eight people across four tracks. SIH teams are **exactly
six**, with at least one female member. Redo the split against six.

## 3. Where does the trained model live?
A pairwise parity classifier, or a taxonomy classifier that *also* closes a named capability
gap. Doing both well is unlikely. The taxonomy classifier is the better answer to "where is
your ML" because it is required anyway.

## 4. What is the Common National Material Code made of?
Mirror the existing NCS structure India already participates in, or an opaque key with
classification held as attributes. Either is defensible. `SMP-000417` with no rationale is
not.

## 5. How honest are we about incumbents?
Naming SAP MDG-M, PiLog and Verdantis, and drawing the line at cross-organisation
harmonization, is stronger than hoping nobody asks.

## 6. Do we build the on-premises path, or only describe it?
A config flag and a local open-weight model make the sovereignty claim demonstrable rather
than aspirational. The published evidence says we lose under a point of F1.

## 7. How many hours do we spend hand-labelling real tender text, and who does it?
Settled that real Indian procurement text is obtainable and unlabelled. What is not settled
is how much of it we annotate.

## 8. Survivorship rules
When five records merge into one canonical material, whose description and whose
specification wins? Standard MDM territory, currently unaddressed.

## 9. Multi-tenancy boundary
Can one CPSE see another's vendors and prices? Politically the most sensitive question on
the list, and we have no answer.

## One correction to carry into the deck

We present the four-way verdict as a design invention. It is better than that: it is a
well-grounded extension of the canonical Fellegi-Sunter model, which has produced three
outcomes with a clerical-review band since 1969. Abstention has a formal name, Chow's rule,
and a modern literature under selective prediction.

Framing it as **grounded** rather than **novel** is stronger with any judge who knows the
field, and it fills the references slide with something real.
