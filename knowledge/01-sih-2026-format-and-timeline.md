## Why this matters more than it looks

Our project plan is written as a **36-hour build roadmap**. That roadmap is for December.
The gate immediately in front of us is a **six-slide PDF** that an evaluator reads in two
to three minutes.

Those two artifacts reward almost opposite things. The build plan optimises depth on one
hard component. The deck is scored on breadth, impact and feasibility, by someone who will
never see our conflict-gate table.

## The idea submission format

Six slides maximum, submitted as PDF.

1. Title slide, team and problem statement information
2. Problem statement / idea overview
3. Proposed solution and approach
4. Technical feasibility and tech stack
5. Impact and benefits
6. Research and references, team details

Guidance consistently says: points, diagrams and infographics, not text-heavy slides.

## Reported evaluation weighting

| Area | Weight |
|---|---|
| Technical approach and feasibility | 25% |
| Prototype / implementation plan | 25% |
| Problem understanding and innovation | 20% |
| Impact and scalability | 20% |
| Presentation clarity | 10% |

Read against this, our plan is strong on technical approach, thin on impact, and silent on
scalability. That is two of the five scored areas with nothing in them.

## Team rules

- Exactly **6 student members**, including the team leader
- At least **1 female member**, mandatory
- All members from the **same institution**
- The college SPOC runs the internal hackathon and nominates teams
- Nomination cap per college: 45 teams (30 software, 15 hardware)
